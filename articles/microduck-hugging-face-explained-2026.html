Meet Microduck: Hugging Face’s $399 Open-Source Robot Duck

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Aug 29, 2026⏱ 9 min read
🦆

On August 27, 2026, Hugging Face unveiled the Microduck — a 25-centimeter-tall, duck-shaped robot that can waddle, pick up objects with its beak, roller skate, and recover on its own after falling over. Priced at just $399, it's designed to make hands-on physical AI experimentation accessible to developers, students, and hobbyists who previously needed expensive lab equipment to work with real robots. This guide explains, in easy English, what Microduck actually is, how it works, and why a small robotic duck is generating so much attention in the AI world.

💡
In one sentence: Microduck is an affordable, open-source robot meant to do for physical AI what free code-sharing platforms did for software — let anyone experiment, build, and share what they create.

What Is Microduck?

Microduck is a small bipedal (two-legged) robot developed by Pollen Robotics, a French robotics company Hugging Face acquired in 2025. Weighing about 800 grams and standing just under 10 inches tall, it's packed with sensors — including a camera, lidar, dual inertial measurement units (IMUs), a microphone, speaker, WiFi, and Bluetooth — giving it enough hardware to sense and react to its environment in real time.

What Can It Actually Do?

  • Walk, waddle, and roller skate using built-in wheels
  • Pick up small objects, like a sock, using its beak
  • Fall down and get back up on its own
  • Follow a laser pointer as a preset behavior
  • Recover balance when someone tries to push it over
  • Learn new behaviors through reinforcement learning, a training method where the robot improves by trial and error

Why "Open-Source" Matters Here

Unlike most consumer robots, which are closed systems you can only use in predefined ways, Microduck ships with its full software stack — including robot control, a physics simulator, and its reinforcement-learning training pipeline — publicly available on GitHub. This means developers can train new behaviors in simulation first, then transfer those skills directly onto the physical robot, and share what they build with the wider community, similar to how open-source software gets shared and improved collaboratively.

The Bigger Company Story Behind the Duck

Hugging Face is widely known as a hosting platform for open AI models — often described as "the GitHub of AI." The Microduck marks the company's continued push beyond software and into physical hardware, following its 2025 acquisition of Pollen Robotics and last year's launch of Reachy Mini, an earlier desktop robot. Notably, this launch comes as Hugging Face is reportedly being considered for acquisition by Nvidia at a valuation around $13 billion — though that deal remains unconfirmed by either company.

⚡
Where you can get one: Initial availability is limited to the US, Canada, the EU, the UK, Norway, Switzerland, Japan, and South Korea — pre-orders are open now, with a charger pack and extra "dev pack" of tools also available for those who want to tinker further.

Who Is Microduck Actually For?

  • Students and researchers learning about robotics, reinforcement learning, and physical AI hands-on
  • Developers who want to experiment with real hardware without the cost of industrial robotics equipment
  • Hobbyists interested in AI and robotics as a creative, tinkering project
  • Educators looking for an approachable way to teach AI and robotics concepts

It's worth noting Microduck isn't designed as a practical home assistant — it can't clean your house or serve as a general-purpose helper. It's explicitly built as a learning and experimentation platform.

A Privacy Consideration Worth Knowing

Because Microduck includes a camera and microphone, and its open-source nature means developers can build custom apps on top of it, it's worth understanding that those third-party apps could potentially access the robot's camera and microphone data, depending on how they're built. Open-source software gives you visibility and control over the code, but it doesn't automatically guarantee that every app built on top of it handles your data responsibly — the same general caution that applies to any camera- and microphone-equipped device.

Why This Launch Matters Beyond a Cute Robot

  • It lowers the barrier to physical AI: real robotics experimentation has traditionally required expensive lab equipment, and $399 changes that math significantly
  • It extends the "open-source AI" movement into hardware: much of the open-source AI conversation has focused on software models; Microduck represents that philosophy extending into physical machines
  • It reflects growing interest in "world models": AI systems that learn to understand and interact with the physical world, not just process text or images
  • It signals continued momentum for accessible robotics, following Hugging Face's earlier Reachy Mini desktop robot launch

FAQ — Microduck Explained

When will Microduck actually ship?

Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics are targeting deliveries before Christmas 2026 for customers in supported regions, based on current pre-order timelines.

Do I need coding experience to use Microduck?

Basic preset behaviors work out of the box without coding, but the primary appeal for developers is training custom behaviors through the open-source reinforcement-learning stack, which does involve some technical familiarity.

Is Microduck the same as a home assistant robot?

No — it's explicitly designed as a learning and experimentation platform for physical AI, not a practical home helper like cleaning or general household assistance robots.

Is my data safe with a camera- and microphone-equipped robot like this?

Open-source software offers transparency into the code itself, but third-party apps built on top of it could still access camera and microphone data depending on how they're designed, so it's worth reviewing any app's specific privacy practices before installing it.

Is this related to Hugging Face's potential Nvidia acquisition?

The Microduck launch isn't directly tied to the reported Nvidia acquisition talks, though both reflect Hugging Face's continued investment in open-source AI, including its expansion into physical hardware.

Conclusion

Microduck represents a genuinely accessible entry point into physical AI and robotics — combining real sensors, open-source software, and a low price point into a small, approachable package. Whether it's picked up by students learning robotics fundamentals, developers experimenting with reinforcement learning, or hobbyists just curious about AI-powered hardware, it lowers a barrier that's traditionally kept physical AI experimentation out of reach for most people.

Cute design aside, Microduck is a meaningful signal of where open-source AI is headed next — out of the browser and text prompt, and into small, tinkerable machines anyone can learn from and build on. 🦆
