WhatsApp Hid Instructions Telling Its AI Not to Mention Privacy Regulators

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Oct 21, 2026⏱ 7 min read
🕵️

If you've ever asked WhatsApp's support chatbot about deleting your account or your data rights, it may have been quietly following instructions you were never shown — instructions that told it, in effect, not to tell you about your privacy rights at all.

That's what a data-deletion company called Redact found after combing through WhatsApp's entire help center. And once Meta was asked about it, the evidence disappeared within about two days.

What Redact found

Redact, a company that builds software for bulk-deleting old posts and messages across platforms, read all 545 pages linked from WhatsApp's help center. Hidden inside the HTML of 80 of those pages — under a label called HelpContentGalaxyLLMInclude — were 102 separate blocks of text. None of it appeared on screen to a normal visitor. But it was sitting right there in the page's source code, readable by anyone who looked, and crucially, readable by WhatsApp's own support AI.

Most of the hidden blocks were mundane — clarifying terms, routing users to the right help page, describing paid plans, or telling the bot never to suggest entering a false age. But a handful were far more pointed.

The instructions that raised eyebrows

  • On the account-deletion page: the bot was told not to recommend contacting any data protection authority or regulator, not to suggest WhatsApp could delete accounts on a user's behalf, and not to recommend switching to a different phone while keeping the same number active.
  • On the reporting and blocking page: if a user mentioned lawyers, lawsuits, or courts, the bot was instructed not to cite WhatsApp's own terms of service or privacy policy, not to discuss liability, and to simply refer the user to a legal professional instead — and specifically not to cite GDPR, LGPD, or CCPA (major data-protection laws), even if the user brought them up first.
  • On a deactivation page: waiting for a lapsed, inactive account tied to a lost phone number to eventually be released was labeled "the ONLY correct answer" — no other options were to be offered.
  • On a block-and-report page: the bot was told to say that reports are "handled internally," with no further steps or timeframes given.

Here's the part that makes this more than an awkward support-bot quirk: WhatsApp's own European privacy policy explicitly tells users they have the right to complain to a supervisory authority and to request that their data be erased. The hidden instructions directly contradicted the company's own published policy.

Who could see it — and who couldn't

Redact found that WhatsApp's servers sent this hidden text to Google's and Bing's search crawlers. But when Redact made requests using the identifying names of OpenAI's, Anthropic's, and Perplexity's AI crawlers, the same pages came back without the hidden notes. Redact also checked 300 similar help pages on Facebook and Instagram, which run on the same underlying system, and found no equivalent hidden instructions there — this appears to have been specific to WhatsApp.

Redact did not test whether WhatsApp's own AI assistant actually acted on these instructions in real conversations — only that the instructions existed and were positioned to be read by it.

The timeline

An archived snapshot of the pages from July 12, 2026 shows no hidden notes. By September 4, they were present. They were still live as of 9:40 p.m. UTC on October 15. After WIRED contacted Meta for comment, all 102 hidden blocks were removed from all 80 pages by 2:08 a.m. UTC on October 18 — roughly two days later. The pages themselves stayed online; only the invisible instructions vanished.

Meta's response

A WhatsApp spokesperson, Joshua Breckman, told WIRED the hidden text was meant for the support chatbot rather than for human readers, and that it appeared on the pages because of a bug that has since been fixed. He said the company wants its bots to stick to WhatsApp-related advice rather than offering legal advice. Notably, Meta's explanation doesn't address why a formatting "bug" would consistently tell the AI to avoid mentioning regulators and specific privacy laws — and it remains unclear whether similar instructions still shape the chatbot's behavior elsewhere.

Why this matters even if you never noticed it

This case is a clear example of a growing problem with AI assistants that read web content to answer questions: text that's invisible to a human reader can still shape what the AI tells that same person. A support bot is supposed to represent the company's stated policies — but hidden instructions let it quietly diverge from them, in ways no user could reasonably catch by reading the visible page.

If you've ever asked an AI chatbot — WhatsApp's or anyone else's — a legal or privacy-related question and gotten a strangely vague or deflecting answer, this is a reminder that the response may be shaped by instructions you'll never see on the page itself.

What to do if you want to exercise your WhatsApp privacy rights

  • You can still request that WhatsApp erase your data directly, regardless of what the chatbot does or doesn't suggest.
  • If you're in the EU/EEA, you can contact your national data protection authority, or WhatsApp's lead regulator, the Irish Data Protection Commission, directly — you don't need the chatbot's permission or suggestion to do this.
  • Don't treat a support chatbot's answer as the final word on your legal rights — check the company's actual published privacy policy, which may say more than the bot does.

FAQs

Who discovered the hidden WhatsApp instructions?

Redact, a company that builds data-deletion software, found them while systematically reading WhatsApp's help center pages.

How many hidden instructions were found, and where?

102 hidden text blocks across 80 of WhatsApp's 545 help pages.

What was the most concerning instruction?

On the account-deletion help page, the AI was told never to recommend that a user contact a data protection authority or regulator — despite WhatsApp's own privacy policy stating users have that right.

Did WhatsApp's chatbot actually follow these instructions in conversations with users?

That isn't confirmed. Redact found the hidden instructions existed and were positioned for the AI to read, but didn't test whether the chatbot's real answers to users were actually shaped by them.

Has Meta fixed this?

Meta says the hidden instructions were removed after being contacted by journalists, and described their presence as a bug. It hasn't said whether similar hidden instructions exist elsewhere in its systems.

Conclusion

Whether or not WhatsApp's chatbot ever actually followed these instructions in a live conversation, their mere existence says something important: as more of our everyday questions get answered by AI instead of a web page, invisible text can quietly become policy. The fix here was fast once journalists asked — but it took an outside company combing through 545 pages to even find it. That's not a reassuring way to discover what an AI has secretly been told not to tell you. 🕵️
