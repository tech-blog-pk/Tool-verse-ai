Amazon Mechanical Turk Is Shutting Down After 21 Years

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Aug 31, 2026⏱ 9 min read
🔌

On August 25, 2026, Amazon announced it will permanently close AWS Mechanical Turk on September 30, 2026 — ending a 21-year-old platform that Jeff Bezos once memorably called "artificial artificial intelligence." For hundreds of thousands of workers around the world, it meant the end of a familiar routine: logging into a portal, completing small digital tasks for a few cents each, day after day. This guide explains, in easy English, what Mechanical Turk was, why it's closing now, and what its story reveals about how the AI industry has evolved.

💡
The irony at the heart of this story: Mechanical Turk workers spent two decades doing the human labeling work that helped train the machine learning models that eventually made much of that same work unnecessary.

What Was Mechanical Turk?

Launched in 2005, Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk) was a crowdsourcing marketplace that connected businesses and researchers with workers — often called "Turkers" — who completed small online jobs known as "Human Intelligence Tasks," or HITs. These included labeling images, transcribing audio and video, moderating content, answering surveys, and sorting data, typically paying just a few cents per task. At its peak, MTurk connected over 500,000 registered workers across 190 countries with companies needing human judgment packaged, essentially, as an API call.

Why the Name Made Sense

The name and Bezos's famous "artificial artificial intelligence" description referenced an 18th-century chess-playing machine called "The Mechanical Turk," which appeared to play chess autonomously but actually concealed a human chess master operating it from inside. Amazon's platform captured a similar idea: many "smart" systems built on top of it appeared automated but actually relied on real people completing simple tasks behind the scenes.

The Shutdown Timeline

  • July 30, 2026: Amazon stopped accepting new Mechanical Turk customers, alongside similar changes to AWS SageMaker Ground Truth and Amazon Augmented AI
  • August 25, 2026: Amazon formally announced the closure, citing a routine internal assessment of its programs and services
  • September 30, 2026: The platform closes permanently

Amazon's official statement was notably brief: "We regularly evaluate our programs, tools and services and make adjustments based on those assessments. Following an assessment, we've made the decision to close AWS Mechanical Turk, effective September 30, 2026." A company spokesperson added only that Amazon remains "focused on delivering the best possible experience for all our Amazon and AWS customers" — with no specific mention of a transition plan for affected workers.

Why Now? The Forces Behind the Closure

  • Specialized AI-native competitors emerged: platforms like Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific built more modern data-labeling services specifically designed for today's AI training needs
  • Data quality concerns grew: a study from Switzerland's EPFL found that 33 to 46% of Mechanical Turk workers were using large language models to complete writing tasks — undermining the very purpose of collecting "human-generated" data
  • AI capabilities reduced strategic need: as AI models improved, many tasks MTurk workers once handled became things AI could do directly, lessening the platform's importance to Amazon's broader AI strategy

⚠️
Worker impact: Krista Pawloski, an organizer with worker advocacy group Turkopticon, told CNBC that MTurk provided "meaningful income" for many workers, even as the platform had been declining for years before this announcement. Amazon has offered no severance or transition plan beyond an FAQ page.

An Unresolved Question: European Workers

Mechanical Turk workers span 190 countries, and European Turkers lose access on the same date as everyone else. The EU's Platform Work Directive, agreed in 2024, gives member states until December 2026 to implement national rules covering algorithmic management and employment status for platform workers — meaning this closure lands just three months before that regulatory deadline. Amazon's shutdown notice makes no mention of European obligations, and the company hasn't publicly addressed the timing.

What This Story Represents

  • The end of an internet-era experiment: MTurk was one of the earliest large-scale attempts to blend human labor directly into automated systems and workflows
  • A symbol of how the AI industry matured: from general-purpose crowdsourced micro-tasks to specialized, purpose-built data-labeling companies
  • A genuine irony: the human-labeled data MTurk workers generated for two decades helped train the AI systems that ultimately reduced demand for that same kind of human labor
  • An open question about gig worker protections: as platforms close with little warning or transition support, it raises ongoing questions about protections for workers in the broader AI-adjacent gig economy

What Happens to Affected Workers?

Amazon has pointed both workers and businesses relying on MTurk to a support FAQ page for guidance on preparing for the closure. Beyond that, the company hasn't detailed specific transition assistance. Workers looking to continue similar work are likely to explore newer AI-focused data-labeling and annotation platforms, which have increasingly captured this market as AI training needs have evolved.

FAQ — Amazon Mechanical Turk's Shutdown

When exactly does Mechanical Turk close?

The platform permanently closes on September 30, 2026, though it already stopped accepting new customers on July 30, 2026.

Why did Jeff Bezos call it "artificial artificial intelligence"?

The nickname referenced how the platform used real human workers to complete tasks that appeared automated from the outside, similar to the 18th-century chess-playing machine the service was named after, which secretly concealed a human operator.

Why is Mechanical Turk closing now specifically?

Amazon cited a routine internal assessment, though the closure follows years of declining relevance as specialized AI-native data-labeling companies like Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific captured much of the market MTurk once dominated.

Is Amazon offering severance or support to affected workers?

Amazon has provided an FAQ page for workers to prepare for the closure, but has not announced severance pay or a formal transition plan for the platform's global workforce.

What replaced Mechanical Turk in the AI industry?

More specialized data-labeling and annotation platforms built specifically for modern AI training needs, including Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific, have increasingly taken over the role MTurk once played in supplying human-labeled training data.

Conclusion

Amazon Mechanical Turk's closure marks the quiet end of one of the internet's longest-running experiments in blending human labor with automated systems — a platform that, in a genuinely fitting irony, helped train the very AI models that eventually made much of its own workforce less necessary. For the hundreds of thousands of workers who relied on it for meaningful income over two decades, the shutdown arrives with little warning and even less support.

As the AI industry continues maturing into more specialized, purpose-built platforms, Mechanical Turk's story is a useful reminder of how quickly foundational infrastructure can become obsolete — and how the humans who helped build that infrastructure often see the least benefit when it's replaced. 🔌
