Anthropic Just Cut Claude Haiku Prices by 90% With the New Haiku 5.5

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Oct 10, 2026⏱ 7 min read
⚡

On October 7, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5, the newest version of its smallest, fastest model family — and the headline isn't just speed, it's price. Anthropic cut API pricing by up to 90% compared to the previous Haiku 4.5, bringing it down to roughly the same cost as OpenAI's cheapest model, GPT-6 Luna, while reportedly beating it on every major benchmark Anthropic published.

💡
Quick summary: Claude Haiku 5.5 now costs just $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for requests under 100,000 tokens — down from $1.00 and $5.00 with Haiku 4.5. Anthropic says this works out to roughly 75% lower costs for typical high-volume workloads, and the new pricing matches OpenAI's budget-tier GPT-6 Luna almost exactly.

What Is Claude Haiku 5.5 Built For?

Haiku models have always been Anthropic's answer to high-volume, repetitive work — the kind of tasks where you don't need the heaviest reasoning model, just something fast and cheap that can run millions of times a day. Think summarizing documents, classifying support tickets, querying databases, or acting as a lightweight "helper" model inside a larger AI agent built on something like Claude Sonnet or Opus. Anthropic is calling Haiku 5.5 its fastest model at standard processing speeds (Opus in its special "Fast Mode" is quicker, but costs more).

The Pricing Breakdown

Here's exactly what changed, per million tokens:

  • Up to 100,000 tokens: $0.10 input / $0.50 output / $0.01 cache reads / $0.125 cache writes
  • Above 100,000 tokens: $0.50 input / $2.50 output / $0.05 cache reads / $0.625 cache writes
  • Old Haiku 4.5 pricing: $1.00 input / $5.00 output — meaning the new base rate is roughly a 90% cut

Anthropic also quietly dropped Sonnet 5.5's cache-read pricing in half, from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens. For subscribers, the company is bundling in monthly API credits too — $100 for Max 5x plans, $200 for Max 20x, and up to $500 shared across a Team subscription.

How It Stacks Up Against GPT-6 Luna

The timing here is hard to miss: Haiku 5.5's new base pricing — $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens — matches OpenAI's budget ChatGPT model, GPT-6 Luna, almost exactly. The difference shows up in the fine print: Luna's higher pricing tier only kicks in after 272,000 input tokens, while Haiku's kicks in much earlier, at 100,000. On the benchmarks Anthropic published, though, Haiku 5.5 reportedly outperforms Luna across the board — including a big gap on computer-use tasks (72.4% vs. 48.9% on one benchmark) and agentic coding tasks (up to 39.2% vs. 16.4% on another, at maximum effort settings). These are Anthropic's own vendor-reported numbers and haven't been independently verified.

It's worth noting Haiku 5.5 still sits well below Anthropic's own flagship, Sonnet 5.5, on every benchmark — this is a budget, high-volume model, not a replacement for serious reasoning tasks.

A Catch: It Uses More Tokens

Not everything about this release is a straightforward win. Some early reports note that Haiku 5.5 uses an updated tokenizer that can consume noticeably more tokens than Haiku 4.5 did for the same task — meaning the real-world savings may end up smaller than the sticker-price cut suggests, depending on what you're using it for. Anthropic's own 75% cost-reduction estimate already accounts for this, but actual savings will vary by workload.

Where It's Available

Claude Haiku 5.5 is live now through Anthropic's own API (model ID claude-haiku-5-5), as well as through AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure. Anthropic also tightened some safety restrictions compared to Haiku 4.5 — for example, it now blocks penetration-testing use cases under the model's standard safeguards.

Why This Matters

This is the third model Anthropic has shipped in the Claude 5.5 family in about a month, and it fits a broader industry pattern we've been tracking: AI labs racing each other down on price for their "everyday" models — the ones businesses actually run at scale — while reserving their flagship, expensive models for harder reasoning work. For developers and businesses building AI-powered products, a 90% price cut on a capable small model can meaningfully change what's financially viable to build.

FAQ — Claude Haiku 5.5 Explained

How much cheaper is Haiku 5.5 than Haiku 4.5?

Anthropic says typical workloads will see roughly 75% lower costs, even though the base per-token price dropped closer to 90% — the difference comes from an updated tokenizer that uses somewhat more tokens per task.

Is Haiku 5.5 as powerful as Claude Sonnet or Opus?

No — Haiku 5.5 is designed for fast, high-volume, lower-complexity tasks. On every benchmark Anthropic published, Sonnet 5.5 scores meaningfully higher.

How does it compare to OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna?

Base pricing is nearly identical, but Anthropic's published benchmarks show Haiku 5.5 scoring higher across computer-use, coding, and knowledge-work tests — though these are vendor-reported figures.

Where can I use Claude Haiku 5.5?

Through Anthropic's own API, as well as AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure.

Can Haiku 5.5 be used for penetration testing or security research?

No — Anthropic added tighter restrictions with this release, blocking penetration-testing use cases under its standard safeguards.

Conclusion

Claude Haiku 5.5 is a reminder that the AI price war isn't just happening at the flagship level — it's happening just as aggressively, if not more so, at the budget tier where most real-world AI traffic actually runs. A 90% sticker-price cut that roughly matches OpenAI's cheapest model, combined with benchmark scores that reportedly beat it, makes Haiku 5.5 one of the more consequential — if less flashy — releases of the week. ⚡
