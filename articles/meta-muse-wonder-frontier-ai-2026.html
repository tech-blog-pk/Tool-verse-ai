Meta Launches "Muse Wonder" — Its Boldest Attempt Yet to Rival OpenAI and Anthropic

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Oct 13, 2026⏱ 7 min read
✨

On October 12, 2026, Meta launched a new family of AI models — Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full — that the company says can compete at the technological frontier with the best models from OpenAI and Anthropic. It's Meta's clearest statement yet that it wants to be seen as a serious player building cutting-edge AI, not just a company that distributes AI through its consumer apps like Facebook and Instagram.

💡
Quick summary: Muse Wonder (previously code-named "Watermelon") is Meta's new frontier-class model family, built for personal and professional AI agents, memory, and coding. Meta claims it matches or beats the latest OpenAI and Anthropic models on its own benchmarks. Two standout features: "Active Memory," which lets the AI remember things about you across conversations, and "Active Focus," which helps an AI agent stick with a goal and adapt when it hits obstacles.

What Makes Muse Wonder Different

Meta is positioning Muse Wonder around three areas in particular: personal and professional AI agents, long-term memory, and coding. Two new capabilities stand out:

  • Active Memory: The model retains information about a user across separate conversations and brings it back up when it's relevant later — rather than starting fresh with no context every time you open a new chat.
  • Active Focus: Built for AI agents that need to complete multi-step tasks, this lets the agent keep working toward a stated goal, try different approaches when something doesn't work, and adapt instead of giving up or looping.

Meta also says the models perform particularly well in specialized professional domains like law, finance, and health, and that they hold up unusually well on tasks the models weren't specifically trained for — which Meta is presenting as evidence of genuine general capability rather than benchmark-chasing.

How It Was Built

Meta says Muse Wonder was trained inside its gigawatt-scale Prometheus data center — one of the company's flagship AI infrastructure investments, and a signal of just how much compute Meta is now willing to pour into frontier model development to catch up with rivals.

The Benchmark Claims

Meta published benchmark results showing Muse Wonder topping or matching the latest models from both OpenAI and Anthropic. As with most vendor-published benchmarks, these are Meta's own chosen comparisons and haven't been independently verified yet — something worth keeping in mind until outside researchers and real-world usage weigh in. Meta didn't offer a direct benchmark comparison against Google's models, though the company has acknowledged it's still working to catch up there specifically.

Where You Can Actually Use It

Availability is tiered:

  • Muse Wonder is available now through the Meta Model API and Meta's coding tool, Muse Code.
  • Muse Wonder Full, the more capable version, is available to Muse Code subscribers.
  • Muse Wonder will also start powering upgraded experiences inside Meta's consumer AI assistant, Muse — initially rolling out to paid subscribers first.

Meta hasn't said whether any Wonder-family models will be released as open-weight models. The company did recently release weights for a separate model, Muse Glimmer, along with tools for running a Muse-based agent locally on hardware — and Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang reaffirmed that the company remains "committed to open source," even if this particular release isn't open.

Safety Measures

Alongside the launch, Meta said it strengthened safeguards against cyber and biological misuse, and added new protections around personal information — relevant given the Active Memory feature's ability to retain user details over time. A model card was released alongside the launch, with a more detailed safety report said to follow.

Why This Matters

This launch matters less for any single feature and more for what it signals: Meta explicitly trying to position itself as a genuine frontier AI lab, going head-to-head with OpenAI and Anthropic rather than just licensing or integrating other companies' technology into its apps. If Meta's benchmark claims hold up under independent scrutiny, it would mark one of the more serious challenges yet to the current leaderboard — in an AI race that, as regular readers of this site know, has been moving at a genuinely frantic pace all year.

FAQ — Meta's Muse Wonder Explained

Is Muse Wonder available to regular Facebook or Instagram users right now?

Not directly yet — it's currently available through Meta's developer API and Muse Code, and will roll out to the Muse assistant for paid subscribers first.

What was Muse Wonder's code name before launch?

"Watermelon."

Is Muse Wonder open source?

Not confirmed. Meta has released other models, like Muse Glimmer, as open-weight, but hasn't said whether Wonder-family models will follow the same path.

How does Muse Wonder compare to Google's models?

Meta didn't publish a direct benchmark comparison against Google, and has acknowledged Google remains a target it's still working to catch up to.

What is "Active Memory" exactly?

A feature that lets the AI retain and recall relevant information about a user across separate conversations, rather than treating every new chat as a blank slate.

Conclusion

Muse Wonder is Meta's loudest declaration yet that it intends to compete directly at the frontier of AI development, not just distribute other companies' breakthroughs through its massive user base. Whether its benchmark claims survive independent testing — and whether features like Active Memory and Active Focus translate into genuinely better everyday AI agents — will likely become clearer in the weeks ahead as developers and users get real hands-on time with it. ✨
