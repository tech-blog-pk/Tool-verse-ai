What Are GPT-6 Sol and Luna? OpenAI’s New Cheaper AI Models, Explained

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Sep 24, 2026⏱ 7 min read
☀️

On September 22, 2026, OpenAI expanded its GPT-6 lineup with two new models — GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna — and led with a simple pitch: the same GPT-6-generation improvements as its flagship Astra model, at half the price. This guide explains, in plain English, what Sol and Luna actually are, what changed, and how they fit next to GPT-6 Astra and rival models like Claude.

💡
Quick summary: Sol and Luna aren't new flagship models — they're cheaper, faster versions of GPT-6 that bring Astra's gains in coding, accuracy, and writing style down to everyday price points. Sol costs $2/$10 per million input/output tokens; Luna costs just $0.10/$0.50.

What Are GPT-6 Sol and Luna?

Sol and Luna sit below GPT-6 Astra, OpenAI's flagship model released three weeks earlier on September 3, 2026. Where Astra is built for the hardest, highest-stakes tasks, Sol and Luna are aimed at everyday and high-volume work. OpenAI says both were trained "with similar methods" as Astra, carrying over its improvements in coding, professional work, computer use, factual accuracy, and autonomous agents — just at a smaller size and lower cost.

Sol is positioned for complex, repeated knowledge work: building features, reviewing code, debugging, and analyzing data. Luna is built for simpler, high-volume tasks with a clear goal, like summarizing documents, extracting information, or answering quick questions.

What's Actually New?

The headline change is price, not a new capability ceiling. OpenAI says improvements in inference efficiency and prompt caching let it cut API costs for both models by 50% compared with GPT-5.6 pricing — and confirmed to reporters that, unlike some past discounts, these are permanent rates rather than a limited-time promotion.

On top of the price cut, OpenAI says both models make noticeably fewer factual mistakes than their GPT-5.6 counterparts on its internal error-tracking tests, and score better on its internal alignment evaluations, including telling the truth more consistently about what they did or didn't check while coding. OpenAI also says Sol and Luna inherit Astra's more direct "collaboration style" — more clarity, less jargon, and shorter answers with fewer low-value details.

Where Can You Use Them?

  • Available now in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu subscribers
  • Free and Go users can access GPT-6 Luna through the ChatGPT desktop app
  • Developers can call both through the OpenAI API using the identifiers gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna
  • Not yet available in regular ChatGPT chat at launch — OpenAI said it would roll out access gradually through the day
⚡
Timing note: Anthropic released an updated Claude Opus 5.5 roughly 90 minutes before this announcement, underscoring how competitive — and fast-moving — the race between AI labs has become in late 2026.

How Do They Compare to the Competition?

OpenAI's own benchmarks claim GPT-6 Sol at its highest effort setting outperforms rival flagship models on business-workflow tests at a small fraction of the cost per task. Independent coverage has noted that Google's cheapest Gemini models remain less expensive than GPT-6 Sol even after upcoming price changes, though the two companies structure their pricing differently, making direct comparisons tricky. As with any vendor-published benchmark, it's worth treating head-to-head performance claims from any lab — including OpenAI's — with a healthy amount of skepticism until independent testing confirms them.

Why This Story Matters

Sol and Luna reflect a broader shift happening across the AI industry in 2026: after a year of leapfrogging on raw capability, leading labs are now competing just as hard on cost and efficiency. For developers and businesses, that means GPT-6-generation improvements — better coding accuracy, fewer factual errors, more autonomous agent behavior — are becoming available at price points that make large-scale, everyday use realistic rather than a luxury reserved for top-tier models.

FAQ — GPT-6 Sol and Luna Explained

Is GPT-6 Sol better than GPT-6 Astra?

No. OpenAI positions Astra as its best overall model for the highest-stakes, most complex tasks. Sol and Luna trade some of that peak performance for dramatically lower cost and higher usage limits.

Which one should I use, Sol or Luna?

Sol suits complex, repeated work like coding, debugging, and data analysis. Luna is built for simpler, high-volume tasks such as summarizing text or answering straightforward questions, at a much lower cost per task.

Are these prices temporary?

No — OpenAI confirmed to reporters that, unlike some past promotional pricing, the new Sol and Luna rates are permanent.

Is there a GPT-6 Terra?

Not yet. GPT-5.6 had a "Terra" tier between Sol and Luna, but as of this launch, no GPT-6 version of Terra exists.

Do I need a paid plan to use these models?

GPT-6 Sol requires a paid ChatGPT Work, Codex, Business, Enterprise, or Edu plan. GPT-6 Luna is also available to Free and Go users through the desktop app.

Conclusion

GPT-6 Sol and Luna aren't about pushing AI capability to a new ceiling — that's still Astra's job. Instead, they show OpenAI racing to make GPT-6-level intelligence affordable enough for everyday, high-volume use, right as competition with Anthropic and Google intensifies heading into OpenAI's DevDay event. For most everyday coding and writing tasks, that price drop may matter more than another capability leap. ☀️
