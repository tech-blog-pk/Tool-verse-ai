How to Use AI in Google Sheets, Docs and Slides: Free and Paid Options (2026 Guide)

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Nov 12, 2026⏱ 9 min read
📊

You don't need to be a spreadsheet wizard anymore. In 2026, AI can write the formula you can never remember, clean a messy column of names, turn a pile of numbers into a chart, and even draft slides. The catch: some of these tools are built into Google's apps but need a paid plan, while others work for free if you're willing to copy and paste. This guide covers all three routes so you can pick the one that fits your budget.

Route 1: The free copy-paste method (works for everyone)

You don't need any add-on. Open a free AI chat (ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude), describe what you want, copy the answer into your sheet or doc. This is how most students and freelancers start, and it's surprisingly powerful.

Formulas

Instead of searching for the right function, just describe the problem in plain words and include your column layout:

  • "Column A has dates, column B has sales amounts. Write a Google Sheets formula for total sales in October."
  • "Column C has full names like 'Ali Khan'. Give me a formula that extracts only the first name."
  • "My VLOOKUP shows #N/A. Here is the formula and a sample of my data. What is wrong?"

Cleaning messy data

Paste a small sample (about 20 rows) and say what "clean" should look like: "Standardize these city names, remove extra spaces, make all phone numbers the same format." Ask for a formula or step-by-step instructions you can apply to the whole column, rather than asking the AI to rewrite thousands of rows.

Docs and slides

Paste your draft and ask: "Shorten this to half the length," "Turn this into a table," or "Give me 6 slide titles with 3 bullet points each." Then paste the result into Docs or Slides.

Privacy tip: before pasting, remove names, phone numbers, ID numbers, and anything confidential. Use fake sample data when you can.

Route 2: Gemini inside Google Sheets and Docs

Google's own AI, Gemini, can live right inside Sheets. You click the Gemini icon in the top-right corner to open a side panel, type what you want, and it previews the change before you accept it. Examples that work well:

  • Create a table for event registrations with name, email, ticket type, and payment status.
  • Write a formula that totals revenue per order.
  • Standardize state names in a column.
  • Build a pivot table and chart from your data.

Sheets also has an =AI("instruction", range) function, so you can classify a whole column with a formula, for example =AI("Tag this feedback as Positive, Neutral, or Negative", A2:A100). Reports say it processes only the first 350 cells per prompt.

The catch: reviews of this feature say it requires an eligible paid Google Workspace plan or a personal Google AI plan, and that free Google accounts don't get this particular set of features. It also works on native Google Sheets files, so convert any imported Excel file first. Availability varies by country, language, and plan, so check inside your own account.

Route 3: Claude inside Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides

Anthropic's Claude for Google Workspace is in public beta on paid Claude plans. It adds a sidebar to Docs, Sheets, and Slides. According to Anthropic, it can:

  • Docs: fix a sentence or restyle a heading without disturbing the formatting, propose bigger rewrites as suggestion cards you can apply or dismiss, and turn text into a table.
  • Sheets: write formulas, build pivot tables and charts, add tabs, and process a range with Python for data cleaning or joins.
  • Slides: build new slides using your deck's own layouts and themes, and flag text that's hard to read or elements that overlap.

How to set it up

  1. Install Claude from the Google Workspace Marketplace.
  2. Open a Doc, Sheet, or Slides file.
  3. Go to Extensions → Claude → Open Claude.
  4. On Team or Enterprise plans, an admin may need to enable it first.

By default it uses "Ask before edits," showing each change as a card you approve. Keep that setting on until you trust it. A reviewer who tested it also noted that Claude can't read or resolve comments in a document, and that you should check every change before accepting it.

Prompts worth trying: "Fix any broken spreadsheet formulas in this sheet," "Clean up any messy data you see," "Turn all this data into a chart," and "Review this presentation and point out weaknesses."

Which route should you pick?

  • Student or beginner, no budget: Route 1. It costs nothing and covers formulas, cleanup, and drafting.
  • You already pay for Google AI or Workspace: Route 2, since it's built into the sheet and saves copy-pasting.
  • You already subscribe to Claude: Route 3, for editing documents and decks in place.

5 habits that make AI spreadsheets reliable

  1. Describe your columns. "Column A is date, B is amount" gets far better formulas than "total this."
  2. Test on a few rows first. Check 3-5 results by hand before applying anything to the whole sheet.
  3. Work on a copy. Duplicate the tab or file before letting AI edit it.
  4. Ask for an explanation. "Explain this formula step by step" teaches you while it helps you.
  5. Never trust numbers blindly. AI can make mistakes, especially on totals and dates. You are still responsible for the result.

FAQs

Can I use AI in Google Sheets for free?

Yes, with the copy-paste method using a free chatbot. The built-in Gemini panel in Sheets is reported to need a paid plan, and Claude's Workspace add-on needs a paid Claude plan.

Is it safe to paste my data into an AI chatbot?

Only if the data isn't sensitive. Remove personal details and confidential figures first, and check your organization's rules if it's work data.

Will AI replace learning spreadsheet formulas?

It speeds you up, but you still need to understand what a formula does so you can spot wrong answers. Ask the AI to explain each formula it gives you.

Does this work for Excel too?

Yes. The free chatbot method works with any spreadsheet app. Just say "Excel" instead of "Google Sheets" in your prompt, since a few functions differ.

Conclusion

Whether you use a free chatbot, Gemini in Sheets, or Claude's sidebar, the skill that matters is the same: describe your data clearly, check the result, and keep a backup. Start with one small task this week, like a formula you usually Google, and let AI handle it. You'll be surprised how quickly it becomes a habit. 📊
