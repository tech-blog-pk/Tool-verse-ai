Black Friday AI Scams 2026: Fake Shops, Fake Reviews and Fake Deals, and 10 Ways to Spot Them

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Nov 21, 2026⏱ 9 min read
🛒

Black Friday is not a scam, but it attracts scammers. Security experts have even nicknamed the days before it "Black Fraud Day," because attacks pile up when millions of people are hunting for deals. What's changed is AI: tools that once needed technical skill can now be run with a simple prompt, so a fake store, a fake review, or a fake "customer support" call can be created in minutes. This guide explains how the scams work and gives you a practical checklist to shop safely, whether you're buying on a big international site or a local online store.

How AI is making shopping scams worse

According to reports from Visa, security experts, and consumer groups, AI helps scammers in a few key ways:

  • Fake stores, fast. Criminals can create convincing retail websites, product photos, and descriptions in minutes. Security firm NordVPN reported (from its own data) that fake shopping website creation rose about 250% ahead of Black Friday in 2025. That's vendor data from last year, so treat it as a guide to the trend rather than an exact current number.
  • Fake reviews. AI can generate hundreds of glowing, similar-sounding reviews.
  • Deepfake endorsements. Fake videos of celebrities or influencers "recommending" a deal appear in social media ads on platforms like Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram.
  • Personalized phishing. Scammers can send thousands of tailored messages that use details from social media or leaked data. Visa executives said that what once required deep technical skill can now be done "with a prompt."
  • Cloned voices and fake support. Voice cloning needs only a short audio sample. Reports have also described scammers getting fake customer-support numbers into AI-generated search summaries, so even "I just googled the number" isn't proof. (Our voice-clone scam guide explains how to defend against the phone side of this.)

The scams to watch for

Based on a NordVPN guide to Black Friday scams and similar consumer warnings, these are the most common:

Scam Typical red flags
Fake shopping website Prices far too low, misspelled domain, no real contact details, copy-pasted reviews
Fake “sale” ad Extreme discounts, countdown timers, payment only by link, crypto or gift card
Fake delivery message Urgent “parcel problem” text, small redelivery fee, shortened link
Fake order or billing alert Confirmation for something you never bought, “verify your payment” link
Fake account alert “Unauthorized login” warning that asks for your password or a verification code
Fake coupon or prize “You’ve won!” offer that wants card details or a survey
Fake travel deal Impossibly cheap flights or hotels, full payment upfront, no verifiable address
Fake charity Vague name, pressure to act now, unusual payment methods
Gift card scam Anyone asking you to pay or share codes by gift card

10 ways to spot a fake deal or shop

  1. Go to the store yourself. Type the retailer's address into your browser or open its official app. Don't buy through an ad or a link someone sent you. Security expert Miles Walker advises checking whether the same deal appears on the real site, since the ads often aren't genuine.
  2. Check the web address carefully. Look for small misspellings, extra words, or odd endings (for example, "brand-sale-online.shop").
  3. Be suspicious of extreme discounts. If a new phone or laptop is 70-90% off, ask why.
  4. Look for real business details. A genuine store has a physical or registered address, clear return and refund policies, and working contact options.
  5. Read reviews on independent sites, not just on the store's own page. Identical-sounding praise is a warning sign.
  6. Search the store's name plus "scam" or "reviews." A quick search can reveal other victims.
  7. Treat urgency as a red flag. "Only 2 left!" and countdown clocks are designed to stop you from thinking.
  8. Notice how they want payment. Gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, or sending money to a personal mobile-wallet or bank account are classic scam signs.
  9. Never share codes. No real shop, courier, or bank will ask for your verification (OTP) code or password.
  10. Don't trust a number you found in a search or AI summary. Find support numbers on the company's official website or on your card.

Safer ways to pay

  • Use a credit card where you can. NordVPN and consumer groups note that credit cards usually offer stronger dispute (chargeback) protection than debit cards. If your region or bank offers virtual or one-time cards, use them for new shops.
  • Prefer cash on delivery or buyer-protected payment when buying from a new local seller, where that option exists, and inspect the parcel before paying if the courier allows it.
  • Set limits. Many banking and wallet apps let you cap daily online spending.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for your shopping, email, and payment accounts.
  • Use unique passwords and a password manager, so one leak doesn't unlock everything.

Delivery and order scams: don't click, check

Delivery scams spike in the holiday season. A text says your parcel is stuck and asks you to click a link or pay a small fee. A "your order has been placed" email shows something you never bought and tells you to log in to cancel. Both are designed to steal your login or card details. Instead:

  1. Open the official shopping or courier app, or type the site address yourself, and check your orders there.
  2. Never click shortened links or open unexpected attachments.
  3. Compare the sender's address or number with the official one.
  4. Ignore any message that asks for your password, card number, or OTP code.

What if you already got scammed?

  1. Contact your bank, card issuer, or wallet provider immediately and ask them to block or dispute the payment. Speed matters.
  2. Change your passwords (start with email and banking) and turn on two-factor authentication.
  3. Keep evidence: screenshots of the site, messages, receipts, and order numbers.
  4. Report it. Use your country's consumer protection or cybercrime authority and the platform where you saw the ad or listing. In Pakistan, check the official website of your national cybercrime agency for how to report.
  5. Watch for follow-up scams. After a breach or a scam, criminals sometimes wait weeks and then try again with a "refund" or "recovery" offer. Don't pay anyone who promises to recover your money.
  6. Don't feel embarrassed. Visa's advice is the same: don't let shame delay reporting.

FAQs

How can I tell if a review is fake?

Look for many reviews posted close together, repeated phrases, vague praise with no details, and no photos from real buyers. Cross-check on independent review sites.

Is a padlock icon or "https" proof that a site is safe?

No. Scam sites can have them too. The padlock only means the connection is encrypted, not that the seller is honest.

Are all Black Friday deals fake?

No. Real retailers run real sales. The safest way to enjoy them is to start from the retailer's official site or app and compare with normal prices.

Should I trust ads on social media?

Be careful. Ads impersonating well-known brands with unusually good deals are a common source of fake-store traffic. Visit the brand's official site directly to see if the deal is real.

Can AI chatbots help me check a shop?

They can help you think through red flags, but they can be wrong and can't verify a seller for you. Use independent checks, like searching reviews and confirming business details.

Conclusion

AI has made scams cheaper to run and harder to spot by eye, so the best defense is a habit, not a hunch. Start from the official shop, distrust extreme discounts and urgency, never share codes, pay in a way that can be disputed, and verify any support number yourself. A few minutes of caution can protect your savings, and your holiday. 🛒
