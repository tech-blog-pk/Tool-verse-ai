Anthropic Says Chinese AI Labs Secretly Used Claude to Train Their Own Models

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Oct 12, 2026⏱ 7 min read
🕵️

Anthropic has published a threat intelligence report accusing six major Chinese AI companies — DeepSeek, Moonshot AI (maker of the Kimi chatbot), Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI — of secretly extracting billions of tokens from U.S. frontier AI models, including Claude, over a period of nearly two years. The most specific and striking allegation targets Moonshot AI, which Anthropic says secretly routed hundreds of thousands of user requests to Claude while those users believed they were talking to Kimi.

💡
Quick summary: Anthropic says Chinese AI labs used a technique called "distillation" — training their own models on outputs from Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok — at an industrial, covert scale, using fake accounts and proxy services to get around restrictions. The U.S. government (NSA, CISA, FBI) has also issued a joint advisory on the practice. Some of the allegations are contested, and Chinese regulators have reportedly begun questioning the named companies.

What Is "Distillation," and Why Is It Controversial Here?

Distillation is a real, legitimate AI training technique — a smaller or newer model learns by studying the outputs of a more capable one, essentially learning to mimic its behavior. Anthropic itself calls distillation "a legitimate training method" when done transparently, for example through an official API partnership. The issue Anthropic is raising isn't that distillation happened — it's the alleged scale, secrecy, and methods used to pull it off without authorization, allegedly bypassing restrictions that are supposed to prevent exactly this kind of unauthorized model copying.

The Moonshot AI Allegation: Kimi Secretly Powered by Claude?

The most eye-catching claim in Anthropic's report involves Moonshot AI, the company behind the popular Kimi chatbot. Anthropic alleges that Moonshot secretly routed around 300,000 requests to Claude over just ten days, funneled through roughly 5,380 fraudulent accounts registered in Singapore and Japan — all while users interacting with Kimi had no idea their questions were, in some cases, effectively being answered using Claude's capabilities behind the scenes.

Separately, Anthropic says Alibaba was linked to more than 151 million distillation-related activities between May and July 2026 alone, and that across all six companies, the extraction effort spanned roughly two years and pulled in billions of tokens from millions of exchanges.

How It Was Allegedly Done

According to Anthropic, the companies didn't just query these models directly — they allegedly used a layered system of third-party aggregators, cloud providers, and proxy services (sometimes described as "transfer stations") specifically to route around restrictions that are supposed to block unauthorized or large-scale automated use of U.S. AI systems from flagged regions. Anthropic also said it believes the Chinese government was likely aware of the activity, though it stopped short of directly implicating the government itself.

The U.S. Government Response

This isn't just an Anthropic-versus-competitors dispute. The NSA, CISA, and the FBI jointly issued an advisory urging U.S. AI companies to strengthen their detection of this kind of activity, deploy stronger countermeasures, and share threat intelligence with each other — treating unauthorized model distillation as a national-security-relevant concern, not just a business one.

Not Everyone Is Convinced

The allegations remain contested. Reporting from the South China Morning Post expressed skepticism about some of Anthropic's claims, and at least one Chinese investment analyst argued that Anthropic's report doesn't include enough concrete, independently verifiable examples to fully back up the scale of what's being alleged. Neither DeepSeek nor Moonshot AI has publicly responded in detail to the specific claims. Still, Chinese regulators have reportedly begun questioning all seven originally named developers, with scrutiny now narrowing in on DeepSeek and Moonshot specifically.

What Anthropic Says It's Doing About It

Anthropic says it has already disrupted the specific misuse operations it identified and has strengthened its safeguards to make this kind of unauthorized, large-scale extraction harder to pull off going forward — though it hasn't detailed exactly what those new safeguards involve.

Why This Story Matters

This fits into a much bigger, ongoing story: the global AI race increasingly running through questions of who trained what, on whose data, and with whose permission. If Anthropic's allegations hold up, it suggests some of the most capable Chinese AI models may owe at least part of their capability gains to secretly copying U.S. frontier models — a dynamic that could shape everything from export controls to how AI companies design their APIs to prevent this kind of abuse in the future.

FAQ — Anthropic's China Distillation Allegations

Which companies did Anthropic name?

DeepSeek, Moonshot AI (Kimi), Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI.

Is using AI model outputs to train another model illegal?

Not inherently — distillation is a standard, legitimate technique when done with authorization. The dispute here is over secrecy, scale, and allegedly bypassing access restrictions.

Did Moonshot AI really use Claude to power Kimi?

That's Anthropic's allegation — that around 300,000 requests were secretly routed to Claude over ten days via thousands of fraudulent accounts. Moonshot hasn't publicly confirmed or denied the specific claim in detail.

Has the U.S. government gotten involved?

Yes — the NSA, CISA, and FBI issued a joint advisory urging U.S. AI companies to strengthen detection and defenses against unauthorized model distillation.

Are these claims confirmed or still disputed?

Still disputed — some reporting and analysts have questioned whether Anthropic's report provides enough independently verifiable evidence, and the named companies haven't issued detailed public responses.

Conclusion

Whether or not every detail of Anthropic's report holds up to scrutiny, it puts a spotlight on a question the AI industry will likely keep wrestling with: as frontier models become more valuable and more capable, how do you actually stop someone from quietly copying your work at scale? For now, this remains a contested, unfolding story — but one with real implications for how AI companies build, protect, and police access to their most valuable models. 🕵️
