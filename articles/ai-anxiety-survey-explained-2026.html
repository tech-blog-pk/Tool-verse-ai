Why More Americans Are Worried About AI Than Excited: New Survey Explained

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Aug 22, 2026⏱ 9 min read
📊

A new Pew Research Center survey released this week found that 52% of Americans now say they're more concerned than excited about AI's growing role in daily life — up sharply from 37% back in 2021, and matching an all-time high. This guide breaks down, in easy English, what the survey actually found, why concern keeps climbing, and what it means for how people are thinking about AI in 2026.

💡
Key number: Only 9% of Americans say they're more excited than concerned about AI right now — the lowest that figure has ever been recorded in this survey.

What the Survey Actually Found

Pew Research Center surveyed 3,488 U.S. adults from June 22-28, 2026, as part of its ongoing American Trends Panel. The core question asked whether the increased use of AI in daily life makes people feel more excited or more concerned. The results: 52% said more concerned than excited, 9% said more excited than concerned, and 37% said they feel both equally.

The Trend Over Time

  • 2021: 37% more concerned than excited, 18% more excited than concerned
  • 2026: 52% more concerned than excited, just 9% more excited than concerned
  • Concern has risen across every age group since 2021, though the pattern of when that rise happened differs by generation

The Big Shift: Young Adults

The most notable finding is about adults under 30. For older Americans, most of the increase in concern happened in the first couple of years after 2021 and then leveled off. But for adults under 30, concern has kept climbing every year since. In 2026, 55% of adults under 30 say they're more concerned than excited — for the first time, a majority of young adults feel this way, and their concern is now roughly on par with older generations who were already more skeptical.

Job Loss Fears Are a Major Driver

Alongside general excitement-versus-concern, the survey found 71% of Americans now believe AI will lead to fewer jobs for humans over the next 20 years — up from 64% just two years earlier. This job displacement worry appears to be one of the central forces pushing overall concern higher, especially among younger adults entering the workforce.

⚡

Why This Data Matters

  • It reflects genuine public sentiment, not just tech industry or media narratives, based on a large, methodologically rigorous survey
  • The under-30 shift is unusual: younger generations are often assumed to be more comfortable with new technology, making this reversal particularly notable
  • It's tied closely to economic concerns, not just abstract fears about AI itself, given how central job loss worries are to the results
  • It may influence policy conversations, as public concern about AI's pace and oversight continues to grow alongside industry investment

What This Doesn't Mean

  • It doesn't mean most Americans have stopped using AI tools — usage and concern can coexist
  • It doesn't mean concern is evenly distributed on every AI-related question; views vary depending on the specific use case, like healthcare versus entertainment
  • It doesn't predict what will actually happen with jobs or AI's societal impact — it measures perception and sentiment, not outcomes

Reasonable Disagreement: Is This Concern Justified?

Some argue this rising concern reflects legitimate, well-founded caution — pointing to real incidents like AI-related security vulnerabilities, job displacement in certain industries, and rapid, sometimes under-regulated AI deployment as valid reasons for skepticism. Others argue public concern may be running ahead of actual near-term risk, shaped more by media coverage and uncertainty than by concrete negative outcomes most people have personally experienced. Both perspectives are actively debated, and the survey itself doesn't settle which view is more accurate — it simply documents how people currently feel.

FAQ — The AI Anxiety Survey Explained

What percentage of Americans are worried about AI right now?

According to the June 2026 Pew Research survey, 52% of U.S. adults say they're more concerned than excited about AI's growing role in daily life.

Why has concern grown so much among people under 30?

The survey doesn't pinpoint a single cause, but job market concerns, rapid AI deployment, and broader cultural conversations about AI's risks are widely discussed as contributing factors among younger adults entering the workforce.

Does this mean fewer people are using AI tools?

Not necessarily — concern about AI's broader impact and personal usage of AI tools are measured separately, and many people who express concern still use AI tools regularly in daily life.

Is concern about AI unique to the United States?

No, though levels vary — some Pew Research surveys of other countries have found lower concern levels compared to the U.S., suggesting cultural and regional differences in how AI is perceived.

What specific job worries do Americans have about AI?

The survey found 71% of Americans believe AI will lead to fewer jobs for humans over the next 20 years, reflecting broad concern about long-term employment impacts rather than any single specific industry.

Conclusion

This Pew Research survey captures a genuine and growing shift in how Americans feel about AI — moving from cautious curiosity toward more widespread concern, especially among younger adults who might have been expected to be the most comfortable with new technology. Job loss fears appear central to this trend, alongside broader unease about how quickly AI is advancing and whether it's being managed responsibly.

Whether this concern proves prescient or overstated will likely become clearer over the coming years, but for now, it's a clear signal that public sentiment toward AI is becoming more cautious — even as the technology itself keeps advancing rapidly. 📊
