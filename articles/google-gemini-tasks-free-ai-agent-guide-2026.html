Google Just Made Its AI Agent Free: What Gemini “Tasks” Does, Who Gets It, and How to Use It Safely

TV
ToolVerse AI
ToolVerse AI Contributor
📅 Nov 19, 2026⏱ 8 min read
✅

For the past two years, AI chatbots mostly talked. In November 2026, Google made a big move to make AI do: it opened its AI agent, now called Tasks (formerly "Gemini Spark"), to free Gemini users in six countries. Here's what it is, how it's different from a normal chat, who can use it, what's safe to hand over, and what to watch out for.

What is Gemini Tasks?

Tasks is an agent mode inside the Gemini app. Instead of replying to one message at a time, you give it a job, and it works through multiple steps in the background, then notifies you when it's done. You can also add instructions while it's working.

Google renamed it from Gemini Spark to make it easier to understand. It was previously limited to paid Google AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers. According to Android Central and Digital Trends (Nov 18, 2026), it now runs on Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen model and is free for free-tier users in the US, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India.

Chat vs Tasks: what's the difference?

Chat mode Task mode
You ask, it answers, you ask again You hand over a job, it works through the steps on its own
You wait for each reply Runs in the background and notifies you when it is done
Mostly produces text Can work across Gmail, Calendar, Docs, Sheets and the web
Good for questions and brainstorming Good for planning, research, briefings and repeating jobs

What can it do?

Examples named in the coverage include:

  • Planning: building a travel itinerary or planning meals.
  • Research: gathering information on a topic across the web.
  • Daily briefings: a morning summary from your email and calendar.
  • Recurring jobs: routines such as repeated checks or regular reports.
  • Google Workspace work: drafting or sending emails, creating and editing Docs and Sheets, and adding several Calendar events at once. (Want to get more out of those apps? See our guide to using AI in Google Sheets, Docs and Slides.)

In the US first, reports say Tasks can also phone local businesses to compare prices or book appointments (it identifies itself as an AI assistant), and prepare purchases through Google Pay within a spending limit you set. You must review an itemized summary and approve each purchase, and receipts are saved to Google Wallet. Some newer features, like texting Gemini or forwarding emails to it, are also US-first.

Pakistan and other countries: Pakistan isn't in the list of free countries in the reports we reviewed. One guide says the feature is available on paid plans elsewhere, but that guide comes from a company selling a competing assistant, so please check Google's own Gemini help page for what applies to your country and account before assuming anything. Availability can also change quickly.

How to start (where it's available)

  1. Open the Gemini app (mobile or Mac) or the Gemini website, signed in with your personal Google Account.
  2. Find the toggle near the prompt box (above it on mobile, below it on the web) and switch it from Chat to Task.
  3. Describe the job clearly: what you want, any deadline, and any limits (for example, a budget).
  4. Let it run. You'll get a notification when it's finished, and you can add instructions along the way.
  5. Review the result. Don't send, pay, or share anything without checking it yourself.

Good first tasks to try

  • "Every weekday at 8 am, send me a briefing of today's calendar events and anything urgent in my email."
  • "Research the three best laptops under my budget of [amount] and summarize pros and cons in a table."
  • "Plan a 3-day trip to [city] with a day-by-day schedule, and add the plan to my calendar."
  • "Create a Google Sheet to track my monthly expenses, with categories and a total row."

Is it safe? What Google says, and what to watch

Google says that web browsing for Tasks runs in an isolated virtual machine, with protection against prompt injection and unsafe sites, and that Gemini asks for confirmation before buying anything. Google also admits Tasks can still make mistakes, and some websites may block it, handing the last step back to you.

Those safeguards matter because agents are exposed to a real risk: prompt injection, where hidden instructions on a web page or in an email try to trick the AI into doing something you didn't ask. We explain it in our guide to prompt injection and how to protect yourself. Sensible habits:

  • Start with low-stakes tasks. Planning and research first; money later, if at all.
  • Keep the approval step on for anything involving payments, sending messages, or sharing files.
  • Set a spending limit if you ever allow purchases.
  • Check the details yourself: dates, prices, addresses, and email recipients.
  • Be careful what you connect. The more of your email and files an agent can read, the more a mistake or trick can cost you.
  • Don't give it private data like ID numbers or bank details unless you fully understand what it will do with them.

FAQs

Is Gemini Tasks really free?

In the six countries named in the reports, yes, for free-tier users. Usage is metered by compute, and paid plans reportedly get higher limits. Google doesn't publish exact free numbers.

What happened to Gemini Spark?

It's the same product with a new name. Google renamed it Tasks and moved it into the main Gemini app.

Can it use my work or school account?

Reports say no. It works only with the personal Google Account you're signed in to.

Will it buy things without asking me?

According to the coverage, no. Gemini asks you to review and approve purchases, and you can set a spending limit.

I'm in Pakistan. What should I do?

Check Gemini's help page and your app to see whether the Task toggle is available for you. If it isn't, you can still use Gemini's chat for free, and the tips here will apply when agents reach your region.

Conclusion

Tasks shows where everyday AI is heading: from answering questions to getting jobs done. It's a genuinely useful shift if you live in a country where it's free, and a preview of what's coming everywhere else. Whatever tool you use, the rules stay the same: start small, keep approvals on, protect private data, and always double-check the result before it leaves your hands. ✅
